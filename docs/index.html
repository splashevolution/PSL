02 / The separation

Three domains. One discipline.

01

Surface

How humans express it.

Words, scripts, diagrams, or future interfaces can offer different handles for the same intended computation.

surface ≠ semantic identity
02

Meaning

What the program means.

A derivation makes identity, relations, actions, context, authority, and exceptions inspectable.

meaning → legal derivation
03

Realization

How machines perform it.

A driver declares what its target can do and refuses a realization when the contract cannot be met.

driver → capability contract

Surface chooses expression. Driver chooses realization. Neither may silently change what it means.

04 / Evidence, not hype

Different evidence answers different questions.

A passing QEMU run is executable evidence. A Lean theorem is a theorem about the model it states. Neither automatically proves the other.

06 / Hardware should age. Meaning shouldn't.

Old hardware should not need to learn PSL.

A thin driver should translate modern semantic intent into whatever protocol the old machine already speaks. If it cannot satisfy the contract, PSL must refuse the realization.

07 / Kill criteria

Make the hypothesis falsifiable.

Failure is a useful research result. PSL should become smaller rather than preserve an attractive claim the evidence cannot support.

01

Independent surfaces only work because their meaning is manually preassigned.

02

A second target forces target-specific concepts into the semantic kernel.

03

A dishonest or incapable driver cannot be rejected.

04

Legacy and modern targets cannot share one semantic program.

05

Proof obligations grow globally rather than locally.

06

Practical complexity outweighs the value over conventional tooling.

08 / Research position
